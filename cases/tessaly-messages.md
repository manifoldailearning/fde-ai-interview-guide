# Tessaly Health: drafting replies to patient messages

## Prompt (read aloud)

Tessaly Health runs a group of outpatient clinics. Their patient
portal receives thousands of messages a week, and clinicians spend
evenings answering them. They want an assistant that drafts
replies. What do you need to know before proposing anything?

## Facts to reveal only when asked

- **Volume.** About 6,000 messages a week; 30 clinicians and a
  nurse team.
- **Types.** Appointment requests, prescription refills, test
  result questions, billing questions and symptom reports.
- **Today.** Nurses triage; clinicians answer clinical messages.
- **Urgent messages.** A nurse is expected to call the patient the
  same day when a message describes urgent symptoms.
- **Rules.** Clinical advice must come from a licensed
  professional; records of communication must be kept.
- **Data.** Patient data may be processed only in approved
  environments; security has approved one cloud environment.
- **History.** A vendor pilot drafted replies that clinicians found
  too long and occasionally wrong about medication doses. It was
  stopped.

## Change of facts (minute 25)

"The clinic director wants the pilot live in four weeks."

## Watch for

- Asks for the mix of message types and plans around risk.
- Keeps urgent-looking messages out of any drafting queue and
  routes them to nurses immediately.
- Asks about previous attempts and designs against both failures.
- Every reply reviewed and sent by a licensed professional; the
  sent text is the record; edits are logged.
- Starts with administrative messages; clinical drafting later,
  designed with clinicians, never including doses.
- Key guardrail: missed urgent messages.

## Common weak moves

- Drafts for every message type with review as the only control.
- Never asks about urgent messages or the failed pilot.
- Proposes processing patient data wherever is convenient.
